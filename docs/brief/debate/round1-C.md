# Round 1 — C: existing tools, no new metamodel

## Position

Leaving the `.spec` language is justified by the dossier's own numbers (Q2): four benches and $52 proved only 9 of Conduit's 19 operations, left 23 clauses `inexpressible`, and required 33 commits to the grammar and generator (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, header, Result). Building valid target pre-states from Malli predicates (`docs/adr/README.md`, ADR-0057, ADR-0068) fails whenever a target hashes passwords, computes slugs, or paginates (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Frame, Journal 2).

What is not justified is replacing `.spec` with a bespoke 18-concept, 29-link metamodel (`dev/spike/metamodel.edn`, `:concepts`, `:links`) encoded in custom EDN files (`rules.edn`, `facts.edn`, `structures.edn`, `interfaces.edn`). Recording HTTP calls against a seeded database and diffing replays is golden-master/approval testing [outside the dossier]—the exact space of tools like Keploy (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 1). Proving rules are exercised is coverage and mutation testing via JaCoCo and PIT (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 3, Result). Typing structures and HTTP routes is OpenAPI (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 13, 14) and consumer-driven contract testing [outside the dossier].

If rebuilt from zero (Q5), specy should drop `metamodel.edn`, `rules.edn`, `facts.edn`, and `structures.edn`, keep database snapshot/restore (`io/db`, `docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 1) and LLM-authored HTTP scenarios, and delegate contracts, assertions, and fault detection to OpenAPI, standard host-language test runners [outside the dossier], JaCoCo, and PIT.

## The three strongest arguments against the current direction

### 1. The specification layer (`rules.edn`, `facts.edn`, `structures.edn`) proves nothing and is already reinventing a broken DSL (Q1, Q2)

**Claim.** All actual verification in the pivot comes from golden-master replay diffs and mutation testing; the bespoke specification layer contributes zero assertions to test verdicts while hitting the exact expressiveness wall that sank `.spec`.

**Evidence.** On Q1, a recorded reference proves only that one concrete input sequence from one seed on one day reproduces the same HTTP status, bodies, and read-back projections (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Result). Whether a business rule is guarded is proved solely by injecting mutants (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 3, 11). The `rules.edn` layer does not judge: "A wrong condition does not make a false red (the recording judges, not the rule): it misclasses a case" (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 10). Nothing verifies a rule's `:says` prose (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 5). Because rules evaluate via first-match (`dev/spike/conduit/rules.README.md`, header), orthogonal update fields break classification: `PUT /user › image` on Conduit had 0 scenarios classed under it because its scenarios also sent `bio` or `username` (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 11), and 16 `too-long`/`incomplete` update rules on Petclinic went unclassed (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 14).

Meanwhile, the EDN condition vocabulary is repeating `.spec`'s trajectory. Starting from equality and order (16 `:inexpressible` rules on Conduit, `docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 9), it immediately added `:matches`, `:length`, `:number`, `:contains`, and integer math, duplicating target regexes and page-size literals while still leaving 5 `:inexpressible` rules on Conduit (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 10), 9 on Petclinic (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 14), and 15 of 48 (31%) on Fineract (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 20). `structures.edn` lacks formats, numeric bounds, and enums (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 16), and refuted 17 claims on Fineract because its five hardcoded types reject `[y m d]` dates and decimal amounts (`dev/spike/fineract/clock.md`, The spec layer)—shapes OpenAPI 3 / JSON Schema handle natively [outside the dossier] (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 14). Leaving `.spec` already sacrificed universal quantification over generated worlds (`docs/dsl.md`, Named states), automatic deterministic boundary edges (`docs/dsl.md`, `inexpressible`; `docs/adr/README.md`, ADR-0058), and explicit `ensure` postconditions (`docs/dsl.md`, `ensure` phrases).

**Consequence.** Maintaining `rules.edn` and `structures.edn` forces continuous language engineering—adding quantifiers, date parsers, and decimal types (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 10; `dev/spike/fineract/clock.md`, Can facts.edn say...)—for a static HTML report (`spike.inspect`, `docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 13) that catches zero bugs beyond raw replay + PIT.

### 2. Recording observations without normative assertions canonizes bugs as contract and destroys reviewability on change (Q3)

**Claim.** Treating recorded observations as the behavioural contract conflates accidental defects with intended behaviour—a structural flaw, not a benign limit—and leaves nowhere executable to state intent.

**Evidence.** The spike concedes: "a spec derived from the observation says what the system does (Petclinic's bugs are recorded as rules), and has no place yet for what it should do" (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 15). On Conduit, 4 scenarios freeze unhandled crashes (`limit 0`, `limit -1`, `offset -2`, missing tag list) as expected bodiless 401s under hollow rule names (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 5). Because Conduit returns a bodiless 401 for every 403, 404, and 500, 23 distinct "not found" and "forbidden" scenarios produce identical recordings (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 5): a rewrite that swaps "not the author" with "unknown article" passes 100% green (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 2). On Petclinic, accepting a null visit date that makes subsequent pet/owner GETs return 500, overwriting rows via ID on POST with 201, and ignoring path `ownerId` on pet updates (204) are all locked in as green reference behaviour (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 14). Furthermore, when behaviour legitimately changes, re-recording produces a 3,600-line snapshot diff with no sub-case unit of review (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 5), losing `.spec`'s line-level `ensure` clauses and `::` departure notes (`docs/dsl.md`, The HTTP surface; `docs/adr/README.md`, ADR-0094).

**Consequence.** If used to judge a rewrite (`docs/dsl.md`, The HTTP surface), the pivot forces the new system to replicate legacy HTTP 500s and bug-for-bug quirks. Intent belongs in OpenAPI schemas (`openapi.yml`, `docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 14) and targeted assertions in standard code-based tests [outside the dossier], where an author explicitly asserts expected status codes and invariants rather than rubber-stamping full JSON dumps.

### 3. Naive double-run recording collapses on clocks, large schemas, unordered lists, and in-memory state (Q4, Q5)

**Claim.** The Fineract experiment proves the pivot's core recording mechanism fails outside small, clockless CRUD apps, requiring infrastructure that mature testing tools already provide.

**Evidence.** On Fineract (`dev/spike/fineract/clock.md`, Replays, Verdict), replaying +1 business day turned 20 of 52 scenarios red (8 branch shifts across "today", 12 clock-derived values like `submittedOnDate` and accrued interest); +35 days turned 32 of 52 red (overdue amounts, plus `0` vs `0.0` type drift). Writing scenario inputs relative to today (`[:today n]`) worsened the result to 37 of 52 red because every echoed date shifted (`dev/spike/fineract/clock.md`, Remedy 2 tried). Even on Conduit, hardcoded binding JWTs expire on 2026-12-02—after which `record` reports "0 unstable" across 100+ broken 401s (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 5)—and session-expiry mutant C1 survived because token expiry sits inside a masked leaf (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 6).

Beyond clocks, Fineract exposed four systemic failures:
1. *Schema scale*: restoring and reading 280 tables took ~16 min per pass, forcing manual restriction to 6 `watched.edn` tables and `:unwatched-tables` for cron jobs and async listeners, with zero detection when a new scenario leaks writes elsewhere (`dev/spike/fineract/clock.md`, Recording).
2. *Unordered collections*: ties on transaction dates caused 3–4 unstable/red scenarios on Fineract (`dev/spike/fineract/clock.md`, Recording) and forced deleting a pet-ordering scenario on Petclinic (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 14).
3. *Non-DB state*: JVM-allocated ID pools survived DB sequence restores (`dev/spike/fineract/clock.md`, Recording).
4. *Missed noise*: JVM identity hashes (`Pattern$Flag;@36f731bb`) required hand-written regex scrubbers (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 2).

**Consequence.** To rebuild from zero (Q5), settling clocks, async tables, set-order matching, and container state comes first—meaning specy would spend its budget re-implementing JVM clock injection (`libfaketime`, `dev/spike/fineract/clock.md`, The target and its clocks; Keploy time-freezing, `docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 1) and custom JSON matchers (like ApprovalTests/JSONassert [outside the dossier]) instead of using them off the shelf.

## What the pivot does that your approach does not

Two mechanisms in the spike go beyond off-the-shelf golden-master, OpenAPI, and mutation tools [outside the dossier]:

1. **DB-diff-triggered black-box API read-backs.** Using database row diffs strictly as a *detector* (`SideEffect`) to schedule pre- and post-call HTTP `Projection` reads—plus reading back resources named in the request even when no table changed (`dev/spike/metamodel.edn`, `:paths` "detect"; `docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 7, 8)—caught silent state corruption purely through the public API. It killed M8, C2, and C8 (password overwritten during profile update while `PUT /user` returned 200) via a login probe, and C3 (`updated_at` mutated on a no-op update) via `GET /articles/{slug}`, killing 22 of 24 Conduit mutants with zero database coupling in the verdict (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 8). Standard approval frameworks and OpenAPI testers do not automatically synthesize follow-up GET probes from dirty DB tables [outside the dossier].
2. **Intra-scenario noise equality classes.** Replacing wildcard masking with per-case equality tokens (`[:spike/noise 1]`, `docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 6) automatically preserved relational invariants across dynamic values ("password after equals password before", "`updatedAt` differs from `createdAt`"), raising C1–C8 mutant kills from 2/8 to 5/8 without hand-written assertions (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 6). Standard approval scrubbers typically erase noisy fields into flat wildcards [outside the dossier].

## A falsifiable prediction

If the pivot is rebuilt from zero and applied to a 25-operation slice of Fineract or KADAI (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 5):
1. Within `rules.edn` and `facts.edn`, either the operator count (`dev/spike/conduit/rules.README.md`, Terms) will expand from 15 to at least 22 (adding quantifiers, date parsing, calendar arithmetic, and set comparisons), **or** at least 25% of documented rules will be marked `:inexpressible` (Fineract's 12-operation slice is already at 15/48 = 31.25%, `docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 20).
2. Deleting `rules.edn`, `facts.edn`, and `structures.edn` while keeping `scenarios.edn` and `reference.edn` will reduce the PIT (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Result) mutation kill score of the suite by **0.0 percentage points**.

## What would change your mind

Two measurements would refute my position:
1. **Cross-schema rewrite verification.** Take a target rewritten in another language with a different database schema (currently a non-goal, `docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Frame) and show `structures.edn` + `rules.edn` + `reference.edn` verifying the new target without re-recording, catching seeded business-rule regressions that OpenAPI schema validation + replaying the HTTP calls alone miss.
2. **Blind defect discovery over JaCoCo + PIT.** On Fineract, show `classify` (`dev/spike/conduit/rules.README.md`, Run) surfacing missing business-rule scenarios that JaCoCo branch coverage (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 3) and PIT mutation survivors (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Result) fail to identify, with under 5% `:inexpressible` rules and zero new operators added to `rules.README.md`.

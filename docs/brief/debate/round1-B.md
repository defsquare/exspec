# Round 1 — B: a model, checked

## Position

A system's behaviour is a state machine: states, a transition relation, invariants, and time as a state variable. Conformance is a check of that machine against the running target, by generating or stepping states, not by replaying a frozen trace. The pivot's oracle is a finite set of recordings; its specification is an ordered list of named facts, first match wins, predicting nothing (`dev/spike/conduit/rules.README.md`, "Writing `rules.edn`"; `dev/spike/metamodel.edn`, concept `Rule`). That is a classifier hung on evidence. It is not a model, and it has no semantics in which a wrong rule is false.

## The three strongest arguments against the current direction

**1. First-match classification cannot refute a rule.**

Claim. The pivot proves that a recorded behaviour was reproduced. It does not prove that a business rule was judged, because a wrong condition never turns a case red.

Evidence. `rules.README.md` states the mechanism: the first condition that holds classes the scenario; the rule "predicts nothing"; "a wrong condition does not make a false red… it misclasses a case" (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, Journal 10). Nothing checks a `:rule`'s text: 23 of 167 recordings are one recording — 401, no body, no effect — including four crashes frozen under a hollow sentence (Journal 5). After five operators, 61 pairs of rules of one operation still share an outcome, 6 rules have more than one outcome, and about 12 cases sit under a sentence that is not theirs (Journal 10). First-match misfits non-alternatives: `PUT /user › image` has no case because a sibling field wins (Journal 11). On the guard campaign, 7 of the 7 unguarded rules are equivalent mutants: the handler fails on a null caller and Conduit answers every anonymous failure with the same 401 (Journal 11). "Not shown: that a condition's boundary is right — every mutant removes or inverts a check, none moves a bound" (Journal 11). The spike's own result: it holds "as an oracle, and not yet as a proof that the rules are exercised" (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, "Result").

Consequence. A specification that cannot go falsely green on its own claims is not a contract. Shipping a widened guard, a swapped 401 rule, or a `:says` that does not match the recording stays green. A transition relation would make those states reachable and the wrong guard a failed post-state [outside the dossier]. Here the only red is a byte-level mismatch with yesterday's target.

**2. Time is not a variable, so date rules are either frozen or checked on one side.**

Claim. The Fineract run bounds the approach to targets whose rules do not depend on a clock, or to a single named day. Holding the clock — the decision already taken — does not lift that bound.

Evidence. Same day, three fresh stacks: 48–49 of 52 green, the rest an order the target does not determine. Business date + 1 day: 20 red. + 35 days: 32 red. No red is a timestamp the noise missed. Eight are branches that crossed today; 12, then 24, are values derived from today, recorded as constants because two stacks on one day never see them move (`dev/spike/fineract/clock.md`, "Replays", "Verdict"). Writing inputs relative to today restores the 8 branches and turns 37 of 52 red, every mismatch a date (`clock.md`, "Remedy 2 tried"). The vocabulary cannot say "the disbursement date is after today": no `[:today]`, no date term, no calendar arithmetic; 15 rules left `:inexpressible` (`clock.md`, "Can facts.edn say…"). `StartingWorldState` is "database rows" and "twin state" (`dev/spike/metamodel.edn`); "the model has no 'the target's clock'" (`clock.md`, "What the shapes and the metamodel could not carry"). Backlog item 12 decides the reference is replayed with the clock held on the recorded day. Remedy 1 of `clock.md` says what that buys: "a rule whose branch crosses today is checked on one side only, forever." The abandoned direction already treated the clock as a replayable input (`docs/dsl.md`, "`now`"; `docs/adr/README.md`, ADR-0118). The challenger named the missing sentence: "due = disbursed + 30 days" cannot be said (Journal 5).

Consequence. Interest accrued, overdue, "not a future date", and any schedule are outside the oracle unless the day is pinned. A mutant that moves a date bound by a week survives every replay on D, because the eight "tomorrow" scenarios are the only ones that cross today, and only when the clock moves (`clock.md`, class (c)). The user asked for this target before deciding to adopt (Journal 20). It is the bound, not an anecdote.

**3. The evidence does not justify leaving a generative contract for a mirror of the implementation.**

Claim. The `.spec` failed where it had to predict a computed value. The pivot "succeeds" by recording what the system does, bugs included, and calling that the specification. Those are not the same claim, and the spike says so.

Evidence. The product being left declares "what a software must do" (`README.md`, opening). A recorded case is one world; a proved operation there was a general statement on drawn worlds (`docs/branches/spike-recorded-cases.md`, "Result"). Named states were generatable registry entries (`docs/dsl.md`, "Named states"; `docs/adr/README.md`, ADR-0030); edges were cases on every run (ADR-0058); fields no clause fixed were a warning of the spec (ADR-0070). The pivot's open question is the opposite: "a spec derived from the observation says what the system does (Petclinic's bugs are recorded as rules), and has no place yet for what it should do" (Journal 15). Those bugs are concrete: a visit without a date is accepted and then answers 500; an id in an add body overwrites the row with 201; the owner in the path is ignored; adding an existing user replaces its password (Journal 14). A rewrite that fixes any of them is red. Intent, as prose `:says`, is unchecked (Journal 5). The Frame deferred the rule layer to "if the first holds"; the Result then says the rule layer is necessary to the promise and "Not built." Extraction friction — building a world from a predicate, predicting an answer — is listed as having "no counterpart here" ("Result"). That friction is the model. Avoiding it is not a proof that a model is the wrong shape. Sixteen of sixteen mutants is circular for M5, killed by a case written after seeing it (Journal 5). Petclinic's 0 unguarded sits beside 33 rules with no scenario and 24 `:same-as` rules standing on another rule's mutant (Journal 14).

Consequence. Adopting the pivot replaces a contract that could disagree with the target with a reference that becomes wrong when the target is fixed. Where intent would go is the machine: invariants and required transitions, against which the recording is a trace, not the oracle [outside the dossier]. The pivot has no such place (`metamodel.edn`, path "specified": claims "declared from the code or derived from the observation").

## What the pivot does that your approach does not

It judges a value the author cannot compute without reimplementing the target. Five rules stay `:inexpressible` because they need the slug of a title or the check of a hash (Journal 10). A model that recomputes the slug is a second implementation; a recording does not need one [outside the dossier]. That independence is real, and it is why the five things the last Conduit spec wrote `inexpressible` — password check, slug, page, counts, a field left out — each became a green case that killed its mutant (Journal 2, Journal 4).

It also separates the writer of scenarios from the writer of mutants and still kills 15 of 16 on the first recording, in 13 min 42 s and 156 k tokens (Journal 2, Journal 4). A hand-written machine can encode the same mistake as the code it was read from [outside the dossier]. The pivot's trace-then-mutate loop does not. Petclinic's recording showed four behaviours absent from the target's tests (Journal 14). I cannot match that without the traces.

## A falsifiable prediction

Adopt backlog item 12 as decided: the Fineract reference names its day and every replay holds `BUSINESS_DATE` at D. Inject one mutant that only moves a bound — the future-date check on submit, approve, disburse or repay, widened from "not after today" to "not after today + 7 days" — and replay the existing 52 scenarios without leaving D. Prediction: 0 of 52 go red. `clock.md` already measured that those branches flip only when the business date moves (8 scenarios, status 400/403 → 200 at D+1), and Journal 11 records that no mutant in the campaign moved a bound. If any of the 52 goes red on D under that mutant, the "one side forever" objection is wrong.

## What would change your mind

A semantics in which `Rule` is a transition — pre-state, event, post-state — not "the first whose condition holds" (`metamodel.edn`), and in which a wrong guard is refutable by a counterexample that goes falsely green, not only by misclassification (Journal 10). Time is a component of `StartingWorldState`, and a Fineract replay at D+35 is green for the 24 class-(b) scenarios by comparing dates as offsets to today, while a mutant that changes "due = disbursed + 1 month" to "+ 2 months" goes red without pinning the day. Until a wrong rule can fail on a state the recording never stored, the pivot is a regression mirror with labels.
